# A Deep Learning Model for Information Loss Prevention From Multi-Page Digital Documents

## Paper Information

**Title:** A Deep Learning Model for Information Loss Prevention From Multi-Page Digital Documents

**Authors:** A. Guha, D. Samanta, A. Banerjee, and D. Agarwal

**Year:** 2021

**Publication:** IEEE Access, Volume 9, Pages 80451–80465

**DOI:** 10.1109/ACCESS.2021.3084841

## Research Problem

Traditional information-loss prevention approaches can depend heavily on predefined rules and access rights. These approaches may have difficulty identifying sensitive information contained within large collections of unstructured digital documents.

## Method / Technique

The researchers developed a deep-learning-based approach for classifying multi-page digital documents. The method uses a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) together with n-gram TF-IDF features to distinguish sensitive and non-sensitive documents.

## Dataset / Tools

The study used a large enterprise document collection from a title-insurance environment. The document population contained approximately 1.6 million documents, with a sample used for experimentation.

## Main Findings

The study demonstrates that machine-learning-based document classification can be used to identify sensitive information within large collections of digital documents.

## Limitation

The approach concentrates on automated document/content classification and does not integrate real-time user behavioural risk scoring or endpoint-level DLP enforcement.

## Relevance to Proposed Research

This research supports the automated data-classification component of the proposed framework. The proposed solution builds on this concept by combining data sensitivity classification with UEBA risk scoring and DLP enforcement, allowing decisions to consider both user behaviour and the sensitivity of the data.
